# Unit-destruction attribution verification

## Objective

Determine which `UnitDestroy` records with no resolved killer can be attributed to
ordinary unit fire, tactical aid, environmental damage, or another exact cause.
Keep unknown causes unknown unless the replay or executable supplies a reproducible
join. In particular, a nearby projectile is a verification lead rather than proof
that it caused a destruction event.

Phase 2's complete area-effect result is recorded in
`findings/unit-destruction-phase-2-area-effects-2026-08-23.md`. The replay explosion
messages contain geometry and effect strength but no actor/source identifier.
Strict same-tick ordering and radius containment therefore remain candidate
evidence only: they narrow 39,935 of 170,954 remaining complete player-unit
unknowns, with 11,118 of those having multiple possible blasts. They do not alter
canonical parser or viewer attribution.

Phase 3's projectile-impact result is recorded in
`findings/unit-destruction-phase-3-projectile-impact-2026-08-23.md`. Treating the
support-projectile vector as constant velocity and projecting it to qualified
explosions is not safe: the first materially useful 10-unit threshold selects
three causes that contradict schema-v17 exact-target controls. Tighter thresholds
have negligible coverage and no validated vector semantics. This bridge is also
candidate research only and does not change parser or viewer output.

Phase 4's self-deletion result is recorded in
`findings/unit-destruction-phase-4-disband-2026-08-23.md`. The actual player action
is `DisbandUnit`, but its client request is absent from all 2,880 corpus replays.
The resulting blink-before-death state is generated by a server routine shared
with other lifecycle paths and occurs before both sentinel and identified-killer
deaths. It cannot support a canonical self-deletion label without a new, distinct
replay-visible signal from a controlled capture.

Phase 5's ordinary exact-target result is recorded in
`findings/unit-destruction-phase-5-ordinary-exact-target-2026-08-23.md`. Without
schema v15's independent equality between the homing projectile's firing unit and
`UnitDestroy.aKiller`, an exact victim-lifecycle target is attack evidence rather
than lethal-cause evidence. The full positive-control pass produced 2,654 wrong
players among 19,364 unique-actor matches and four wrong teams among 19,708
unanimous-team matches. It would reach only 175 of 170,954 remaining unknown
complete-unit deaths at team level. Player and team attribution are both rejected;
sentinel-512 deaths remain unknown.

Phase 6's deterministic lifecycle result is recorded in
`findings/unit-destruction-phase-6-lifecycle-contexts-2026-08-24.md`. Ghidra shows
that the building-resident and container-occupant fatal paths copy the initiating
killer and damage-source context into each child death and generate an exact finite
terminal-direction signature. Combined with exact active-generation occupancy or
type-2 relations, same-raw-tick terminal events, teardown ordering, and killer
agreement, this supports 14,723 canonical `buildingCollapse` and 2,779
`destroyedWithContainer` contexts. Context does not identify a building attacker.
Only 67 container children inherit tactical-aid cause, because only those parents
have independent schema-v17 exact-target support/team evidence. Canonical unknown
complete-unit causes fall from 213,539 to 213,472; the other contexts improve loss
semantics without inventing causality.

Phase 8's forced-death result is recorded in
`findings/unit-destruction-phase-8-forced-death-callers-2026-08-24.md`. All six
direct callers of the server's forced-death helper are classified; five cannot
produce a multiplayer unit loss and the sixth is the unserialized bridge and
destroyable-path kill box. The self-deletion caller split is complete: the blink
scheduler records a reason byte at unit `+0x128`, but that byte is not serialized,
defaults to the disband value, and its branch ends in `UnitRemove` rather than
`UnitDestroy`. Phase 8 also corrects the terminal-direction signature's meaning to
"fatal damage with no direction vector"; it is not exclusive to the forced and
inherited helpers. No parser or viewer output changes.

### Bounded parser rule — timeline schema v15

The parser does not use a historical-owner fallback. It records each active unit
lifecycle from `UnitCreate` until `UnitRemove`, replacement, or `UnitDestroy` and
captures firing player/team at `ProjectileHomingUnitCreate`. An otherwise
unresolved non-sentinel destruction is attributed only when an earlier projectile
is at most 0.5 seconds old, its `aUnit` equals `UnitDestroy.aKiller`, its `aTarget`
equals the destroyed `aUnit`, and that target still has the same creation-offset
generation. All qualifying projectiles must agree on one actor. Future projectiles,
missing unit state, target reuse, conflicting actors, and `aKiller=512` abstain.

The native corpus gate parses all 2,865 accepted replays at schema v15 and retains
the same 15 established corrupt/empty rejections. All 16 private ground-truth
fixtures pass. This rule establishes the actor associated with the serialized
killer-unit bridge; `UnitDestroy` still carries no projectile ID, so it does not
claim a more specific projectile-impact identity.

Timeline schema v17 promotes only sentinel-512 deaths where the homing support

Its timing/signature therefore cannot distinguish deletion and is not promoted.

### Multi-victim tactical-aid presentation requirement

One tactical-aid deployment can destroy several complete units, including units
owned by different players. This applies to large area effects such as tactical
nukes and carpet bombing and can also occur with well-placed tank busters and other
smaller strikes. Canonical attribution must remain per destroyed unit and preserve
each victim owner. The viewer should list every attributed unit loss after the TA
deployment; it must not collapse the strike into one generic destruction row or
show only one affected player. Temporal grouping may organize presentation later,
but it cannot replace the individual destruction facts.

unit loss:

- `0x004ddf20` `EXG_Spawner::Spawn()` forces death only when the spawned unit's type
  has zero maximum health; all 122 shipped multiplayer definitions have a non-zero
  `myHealth`, so the branch is dead.
- `0x00513260` is `EXG_SelfDestruct::Update`; the `SelfDestruct` parasite hash appears
  only on two ICE section-separator entries, never on a playable unit.
- `0x00506bd0` is `EXG_Blower::Update`; the corpus contains zero `BlowerBlew` records.
- `0x004a6ac0` is slot 4 of the `EXG_Game` debug/cheat interface whose sibling
  `CreateUnit` is cheat-gated; it validates no ownership and has no replay companion.
- `0x00536670` is the script-driven `WICG_FortificationPoint` unfortify, a typed
  fortification loss rather than a player unit kill.
- `0x004d37c0` `EXG_UnitContainer::KillAllUnitsInArea()` is the only live gameplay
  caller, reached from `WICG_Bridge` and `WICG_GameObject_Destroyable_Path`
  destruction. Its kill box is runtime state and is not serialized.

The self-deletion caller split is also complete. Blink scheduler `0x004ca3e0` takes a
reason argument and stores it in unit byte `+0x128` when that byte is still zero:
disband passes 0, `EXG_Game::PlayerSetRole` and `EXG_PlayerContainer::RemovePlayer`
pass 1, and `EXG_Game::PlayerJoinTeam` passes its request argument. `BlinkUnit`
carries constant blink time and frequency on every path, the byte defaults to zero so
disband is indistinguishable from "unset", and its only reader,
`EXG_UnitContainer::Update` `0x004d7d20`, uses it solely to decide a refund. That
branch ends in `UnitRemove`, not `UnitDestroy`, so disband, team change, role change,
and player departure are not part of the unattributed destruction population.

Phase 8 also corrects the meaning of the terminal-direction signature. `0x004cc280`
has a third caller, `0x004ce830`, which feeds the *ordinary* damage routine. Its four
callers cover collision crush (real killer ID), blower splash, building damage
splashed onto residents, and every negative `EXG_Unit::ChangeHealth` including support
clouds and per-tick attrition. The signature therefore identifies fatal damage that
carried no direction vector, not a specific mechanism. The Phase 6 rules remain sound
because they additionally require exact slot or relation binding, same-raw-tick
terminals, and killer agreement.

## Phase 9 — death-explosion blasts (2026-08-24)

Phase 9 closes the last bounded hypothesis Phase 8 left open, and refutes it. A
sentinel killer combined with a directional hit vector is **not** a tactical-aid
signature.

`EXG_Unit::Kill` `0x004ccef0` (`.\EXG_Unit.cpp:0x4e0`) asserts
`aKillerUnit <= EX_MAX_UNITS`, stores the killer as the `u16` at unit `+0x234`,
and gates both scoring calls on `killerUnitId < 0x200`. The sentinel `512` is
therefore the engine's own encoding for "this damage had no owning unit", not a
lost or unresolved value, and the engine itself declines to award such a kill.

`EXG_BlastContainer` applies queued blasts at `0x004f2b50`
(`.\EXG_BlastContainer.cpp`, assert `aBlastRadius > 0.0f` at line `0x3a`) and
passes the **literal** `0x200` as the killer for every blastable object in
radius, together with a real world position that yields a genuine hit direction.
Blasts are enqueued only by `0x004f2df0`, whose single caller at `0x0051350d`
sits in the `.\EXG_Death.cpp` method `0x00513450`, which reads the shipped
`myBlastRadius` and `myDamage` type properties immediately beforehand.
`EXG_BlastContainer::Update` `0x004f2e60` drains exactly one blast per tick from
the `EXG_Game` tick `0x004b2970`, container at game offset `0x13200c` (only four
references to that offset exist in `.text`, all in `EXG_Game`).

`EXG_Unit::TakeDamage` `0x004d2bd0` has exactly one reference in the image, the
DATA reference at `0x007738b0` = `EXG_Unit` vtable `0x00773864` + slot 19
(`0x4C`), confirming virtual-only dispatch.

Corpus measurement (`scripts/death_explosion_chain_scan.py`, 2,880 replays, 0
failures) confirms the predicted temporal signature: sentinel directional deaths
share a tick with another death 50.4% of the time against 10.2% for the
attributed directional control (4.93x), and follow one within 0.25 s 46.4% against
13.7% (3.38x). The enrichment is specific: in the synthetic-direction class the
sentinel and attributed rates are indistinguishable (59.9% vs 62.1% same tick),
ruling out a generic "deaths cluster in time" artefact.

This proves blast is *a* producer of the class, which is sufficient to refute the
support hypothesis. It does not prove blast is the *only* producer, and it gives
no per-death parent: `UnitDestroy` serializes no position, so the `myBlastRadius`
half of the mechanism cannot be evaluated from the replay stream. The population
therefore still has no deterministic per-death actor or team, and must continue
to be reported as an unattributed loss.

Address to compilation-unit attribution used throughout Phase 9 is reproducible
via `scripts/wic_source_map.py`, which derives function entries from rel32 call
targets and source files from assert-string push sites, with no Ghidra code
indexing.

## Phase 10 — the buildingDamage context is rejected (2026-08-24)

`0x004da180` handles building damage: it subtracts damage from building health
at `+0xd0`, records the killer unit at `+0x10c` when the building becomes lethal,
and then splashes onto residents through `0x00517f10` when building state
`+0x168 != 3`. `0x00517f10` walks the resident array (count `+0x4c`, entries
`+0x58`, unit id at entry `+8`) and calls `FUN_004ce830(damage, p, q, 0)`. The
final argument is a literal `0`, and `0x004ce830` branches on exactly that:

```c
uVar2 = (param_4 == 0) ? 0x200 : *(undefined2 *)(param_4 + 0xc0);
uVar1 = FUN_004cc280(0, param_2, param_3);   /* synthetic direction */
```

The building's attacker is therefore in hand at the call site and deliberately
discarded. Resident splash deaths are by construction sentinel killer **and**
synthetic direction — the same "no actor by design" shape as the Phase 9 blast
result.

That constraint refutes the candidate rule deterministically. A genuine splash
death must be sentinel-synthetic, yet the rule *victim was a resident of a
building that lost health in the same raw tick* fires across the corpus
(`scripts/building_damage_context_audit.py`, 2,880 replays, 0 failures) on:

| class | residents | fires | rate | can be splash? |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---|
| sentinel + synthetic | 22,276 | 1,569 | 7.04% | yes |
| sentinel + directional | 20,084 | 1,127 | 5.61% | no, wrong direction class |
| attributed + directional | 56,755 | 814 | 1.43% | no, killer resolved |
| attributed + synthetic | 15,028 | 535 | 3.56% | no, killer resolved |

It fires 2,476 times where it is provably wrong against 1,569 times where it
could be right, so a majority of matches are false by construction. Buildings
under attack are simply dangerous, and same-tick co-occurrence carries no causal
information. Even a perfect rule would have yielded 1,569 deaths (0.2% of all
complete-unit destructions) carrying no actor and no team. Rejected on both
determinism and yield; closed.
